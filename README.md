# A Frozen Sketch Catalogue, Audited: Persistence of Hand-Drawn Scar Identifications in the Blue Spring Manatee Aggregation, 2013–2026

Sirenian conservation / data rescue · 2026

Author: Atharva Chourey
Affiliation: Atharva Chourey Lab (independent research lab)

*Manuscript — not yet peer-reviewed.*

## Abstract

Photo-identification of Florida manatees (*Trichechus manatus latirostris*) is maintained in the Manatee Individual Photo-identification System (MIPS), which ingests photographs. Alongside it, the long-running Blue Spring study maintained a parallel, hand-drawn "Practical Scar Sheet" catalogue — pen-and-ink sketches of each animal's scar pattern — which was frozen in April 2013 and never audited. We transcribed the complete frozen set (210 entries, 209 with catalogue IDs), pre-registered six predictions — including the sheet's own 3–5-year and 10-year "drop rules" treated as falsifiable claims about re-identifiability — and joined the transcription to every public post-2013 record we could source. The public record reaches only 21.5% of the catalogue, so the pre-registered fallback applies: we report a coverage audit and a non-representative case series. Within the joined subset (n = 45), 34 animals were publicly re-identified after 2013 with a median last record in 2025, twelve years past the freeze; known deaths account for 24.4%; scar load did not predict re-identification (HIGH 69.7% vs LOW 66.7%), and the pre-1990 cohort persisted at least as well as the 2000–2013 cohort (86.7% vs 82.4%) — the reverse of the registered prediction. Two individuals first identified in the 1970s were still being recognised in 2026. The drop rules are thus far too pessimistic for every animal the public record can see, and untested for the silent majority; resolving the latter requires the MIPS join, which we have formally prepared.

*Keywords:* Florida manatee; photo-identification; scar catalogue; Blue Spring; data rescue; pre-registration; MIPS

## Key results

- **Coverage failure is the headline, reported as registered** — the public ruler joined only 45 of 209 ID-bearing entries (21.5%), below the pre-registered 40% fallback line, so the paper is a coverage audit plus a non-representative case series — not a population estimate. It does not claim more.
- **Where visible, identities persist far past the drop rules** — median last public record 2025, twelve years after the April-2013 freeze; animals first identified in 1970 and 1974 were still recognised in the 2025–26 season, past the sheet's own 10-year drop.
- **Registered scorecard: 2 pass / 3 fail / 1 void** — scar load showed no re-identification gradient (P3 failed), and cohort age ran in the reverse of the registered direction (P5 failed). Failures are reported as failures.
- **The catalogue itself is the rescue** — the complete frozen set (210 entries) was transcribed in two independent passes with adjudication; the transcription, ruler, and join table are preserved with the paper.
- **The decisive join is named, not hidden** — the remaining 78.5% is unverifiable from outside the researcher-only MIPS archive; a formal data request to the MIPS custodians is prepared and is the study's main forward path.
